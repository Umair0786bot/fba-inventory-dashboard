Decision engine — what discount clears the stock and recovers the most cash?
The model, in one line: a discount lowers the price you collect but lifts sell velocity, so the aged stock clears sooner and stops accruing the surcharge. Net recovery counts only the cash from units you actually sell inside the horizon, minus the surcharge you actually pay — units still sitting at the end are neither credited nor written off (they're shown as Left).
Guard-rails per SKU: Ceiling is the deepest discount that still returns 100% of product cost — its price is the COGS-recovery price = (COGS + FBA) ÷ (1 − referral). Floor is where the cash payout hits zero; past it you pay Amazon to take the unit. ★ Best scans every discount from 0–90% and picks the one with the highest net recovery for the scope.
Velocity is measured, not guessed: units/day comes from each SKU’s real daily sales history (the KPI tracker’s 26-day per-SKU series), falling back to the workbook’s L30 velocity where a SKU has no history — the Src column shows which. The response to a discount uses a constant-elasticity curve (multiplier = (1 ÷ (1 − discount))^E), so a 10% cut moves velocity only slightly while a deep cut moves it a lot. The Target velocity view shows, per SKU, the pace required to clear the cohort inside the horizon, the gap from today, and the discount that would close it.

Target monitoring — SV & DOH
Current SV = units sold ÷ days elapsed (actual pace). Current DOH = remaining units ÷ current SV (days to clear at today's pace). Target SV = remaining ÷ coverage days (pace required). Target DOH = coverage window. Uplift needed = Target SV − Current SV, shown absolute (units/day) and % over current. 🔴 behind = current pace won't clear within the coverage window.
