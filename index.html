Aged stock — what is it costing us, and what is the cheapest way out?

New here? Read this first.

Stock that has sat in Amazon's warehouse for 181 days or more gets hit with a monthly aged-inventory surcharge (AIS). It is charged every month the stock keeps sitting, and it gets steeper the longer it stays. Doing nothing is not free — that is the whole point of this tab.

You have four ways out. This tab prices all four with your real numbers and tells you which one keeps the most cash:

⏳ HoldChange nothing. Keep selling at full price and keep paying the surcharge.
DiscountCut the price so it sells faster. You earn less per unit but escape surcharge.
💵 LiquidateAmazon sells it to a bulk liquidator. You get a small cheque, the surcharge stops today.
🚚 RemovePay Amazon to ship it back to you. Costs money, but the stock is yours again.

Every figure below is judged on net lifetime — all the cash you collect minus all the surcharge you pay, from now until that stock is finally gone. Work down the four steps in order.

1Where do we stand? How much aged stock we are carrying and what it is costing us if we do nothing.
2What are our options worth? The same stock priced five different ways. The winner is highlighted.
Read the Net recovery column — that is cash collected minus surcharge paid, for each option. The row with the biggest number is the best plan for the book as a whole. Do nothing is the baseline; the last column shows how much better or worse each option is than that.

Best % is found per SKU by testing every discount from 0% to 90% and keeping the one that leaves the most cash after the surcharge. It is not capped at the ceiling — it often lands lower, because once a smaller cut already clears the stock, going deeper only gives away margin.
3Set the plan Pick which discount rule to run, then read the summary in plain English.
Discount basis is the main control. Flat % applies one percentage to everything. Ceiling is the deepest cut that still returns your full product cost. Floor is where your payout hits zero. Best % lets the app solve the ideal percentage for each SKU separately — usually the one to use. Everything below this point updates as you change it.
💵 FBA Liquidations — what the stock is worth if we hand it to a liquidator

Amazon sells the units to a wholesale liquidator and pays us what survives two deductions: gross recovery value (the liquidator's bid) less a liquidations referral fee charged on that gross value, less a per-unit processing fee banded on size tier and weight. net = GRV − referral − processing, floored at zero — if the fees swallow the bid there is no payment, but Amazon does not bill the difference back.

Every rate here is an input, not a constant — the rate card lives behind a Seller Central login, so paste the live numbers in and the whole Engine reprices on the spot. Defaults are the published US card ($0.25 at the smallest standard band up to $1.90 + $0.20/lb over 10 lb, with a 15% referral fee on gross recovery value). Weight comes from column K of 1 Year Projections; SKUs with no weight on file are priced at their tier's opening band and flagged in the table, so treat those as a floor.
One caveat on REMOVE. This model prices removal the way the rest of the workbook always has — as a pure cost, with no value credited to the goods once they are back in the warehouse. Liquidation pays cash instead of charging a fee, so on this book REMOVE can only ever win when the liquidation bid nets below zero, and the ACTION column will rarely show it. If you have a genuine resale channel for pulled stock, that residual value is not in here and removal is worth more than the table says.

Per-SKU detail

Everything decides on lifetime Every verdict here — Winner, ACTION, Liquidate, Remove, Rec % — is solved on Net lifetime: cash minus surcharge until this stock is actually sold through — run to true clearance, not truncated at 36 months. AIS 1yr is shown for budgeting only and decides nothing.
Every column header explains itself on hover. Ceiling = the deepest discount that still returns 100% of product cost. Floor = the point where your payout is exactly zero; past it you pay Amazon out of pocket to move the unit. Payout/unit = deal price − Amazon referral − FBA fee (product cost is already spent, so it is not deducted twice). Target SV = the monthly pace needed to clear this SKU's aged stock within a year. To clear figures run the surcharge all the way until the stock is gone — the honest number for SKUs that need more than a year, and the reason holding usually loses.

Steps 1–4 already give you a recommendation. This section shows the solver that produces the Rec %, and lets you change what it assumes about ad spend, coupon costs and how much faster a discounted product sells. Change nothing here and the defaults apply.
Discount optimizer — the percentage is solved for, not chosen
Referral % is the one on the control row at the top of this tab, so the optimizer and the scenarios always agree. Elasticity is m(d) = 1 + 5d below 20% off and 10d at or above it, with k scaling the uplift only — so 0% off is always exactly 1× base, whatever k is.

Elasticity calibration — measured k per parent

Parent ? SKUs v0 pooled /mo ? v observed /mo ? Days ? Live % off ? k measured ? Status ?
Measurement hygiene: at least 14 days of units/day, stockout and badge-off days excluded, and PPC held constant across the window — otherwise the uplift is jointly caused and k comes out overstated. A calibrated k replaces Base for that parent, and the Conservative/Aggressive band re-centres to ±40% of it.
Rec % is the discount that maximises total recovered cash = unit cash × units sold − LTSF drag while clearing + exit value of whatever is left, found by testing every percentage on the grid up to the cap. It is a solved number, not a judgement call. Click any row in the table below to see why that number won.

Target monitoring — SV & DOH
Current SV = units sold ÷ days elapsed (actual pace). Current DOH = remaining units ÷ current SV (days to clear at today's pace). Target SV = remaining ÷ coverage days (pace required). Target DOH = coverage window. Uplift needed = Target SV − Current SV, shown absolute (units/day) and % over current. 🔴 behind = current pace won't clear within the coverage window.

Per-SKU deal detail
% off is the real discount your deal price represents against the current list price. Payout/unit is what lands in your account at the deal price: deal − referral − FBA. Margin/unit subtracts product cost on top, so it is the true profit per unit sold at this deal.
Ceiling / Floor / Best are the same three scenarios as the Decision Engine, shown here as the percentages and the prices they imply — so you can see at a glance whether today's deal price is leaving room on the table or already past the point where each unit costs you cash. Headroom is Best % − your deal % : positive means the maths says you could go deeper and still recover more.
SV now → at deal is today's measured monthly pace and the pace this discount is expected to produce; Target is what would be needed to clear the aged stock inside a year. The spark line is that SKU's real daily sales.
